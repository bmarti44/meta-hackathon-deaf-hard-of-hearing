**Executed analysis: how much evidence is behind the promising SRT number?** Code: [analyze.py](../research/unstick2/analyze.py); manually transcribed, source-located inputs: [inputs.json](../research/unstick2/inputs.json); full outputs: [results.json](../research/unstick2/results.json). For the complete-pair summary I computed `mean ± t(0.975,9) × SD/sqrt(n)`, and then added explicit hypothetical outcomes for the two incomplete cases using sums and sums of squares. These are **new exploratory aggregate-data sensitivity calculations**, not the authors' Wilcoxon analysis, a validated responder analysis, or a new clinical experiment.

| Analysis | Mean benefit, dB | Approximate 95% t interval | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Complete pairs | 5.900 | **[3.182, 8.618]** | Above 3 under the small-sample t assumptions. |
| Both missing cases assigned 0-dB benefit | 4.917 | **[2.290, 7.543]** | Point estimate remains >3; lower interval does not. |
| Both assigned 3-dB benefit | 5.417 | **[3.118, 7.715]** | Threshold clearance requires a favorable missing-case scenario. |
| Both assigned −5-dB benefit | 4.083 | **[0.614, 7.553]** | A failure-inclusive implementation estimand has much less precision. |

The complete-case two-sided test of mean=3 gives **p=.0390**, unadjusted. This is not the published test of zero and not family-wise evidence. Rounded input uncertainty alone puts the lower bound at **3.096–3.267 dB**. Thus rounding is not the main problem. Generalization is: if an unknown comparator/transport penalty is `b`, the point estimate retains ≥3 until `b=2.9 dB`, but the 95% lower bound retains ≥3 only until **b=0.182 dB**. A 0.25-dB penalty already removes the latter property. This is a sensitivity equation, not an estimate of the actual penalty. **Do not subtract the separate 4.2–4.3-dB ForwardFocus result**: participants, geometry and processing differ.

Normal-theory inference at this N is fragile; raw paired differences are needed for robust shifted-threshold inference. Assigning zero to missing cases is a scenario, not a claim about why they were missing. The result does justify spending effort on a reproducible adult comparator experiment; it does not establish a transferable ≥3-dB reserve. No Bayesian or frequentist probability here is a joint promotion probability.

**Executed analysis: is the cited network a streaming candidate at all?** The new paper reports 1-ms algorithmic look-ahead using an impulse analysis and processes test mixtures offline. It identifies [Keles's public checkpoint/model card](https://huggingface.co/cankeles/ConvTasNet_WHAMR_enhsingle_16k). The card specifies a 16-sample encoder kernel, 8-sample stride at 16 kHz, eight blocks and three repeats; it does **not** specify `causal` or `norm_type`. The inspected [upstream constructor](https://github.com/asteroid-team/asteroid/blob/master/asteroid/models/conv_tasnet.py) defaults to `causal=False`, `norm_type="gLN"`. The defaults are also documented in [Asteroid v0.5.0](https://asteroid.readthedocs.io/_/downloads/en/v0.5.0/pdf/), so this concern is not inferred solely from a recent code change.

I enumerated the dependency graph for the corresponding symmetric three-tap dilated convolutions. With dilations 1…128 repeated three times, the maximum rightward dependency is `3 × (1+2+…+128) = 765` latent frames. At the card's stride/rate this is **382.5 ms of convolutional future context**, before considering encoder/decoder support. Moreover, [global layer normalization](https://github.com/asteroid-team/asteroid/blob/master/asteroid/masknn/norms.py) uses the entire supplied time segment. If enabled, it introduces segment-wide dependence. The [convolution implementation](https://github.com/asteroid-team/asteroid/blob/master/asteroid/masknn/convolutional.py) supports both causal and symmetric modes; the calculation specifically assumes the latter.

**This is a conditional architecture finding, not a measurement of the study checkpoint.** The serialized model arguments and the study's software version were not obtained. Do not accuse the study of a proven erroneous latency value or declare J2 killed. The strongest justified conclusion is that encoder-window length and the inspected model card do not establish the full-network latency claim. This uncertainty is immediately testable and potentially decisive.

I also ran a seeded, synthetic nonlinear masking experiment to test the sufficiency of the proposed evidence. A multiplicative gate using global statistics produces **zero pre-echo** on an isolated impulse, yet changing only the future suffix changes outputs in the common past by a maximum **0.5633 normalized amplitude**, including samples more than the nominal 16-sample allowance before the suffix. A cumulative-statistics causal control produces **zero** common-prefix change. Both have identical impulse-onset timing. This proves by counterexample that an apparently prompt impulse output is insufficient evidence of causality. It does not measure this DNN's speech quality, weights or latency.

J3 already asks for a shift test and static audit; the recommendation is to make their implementation explicitly adversarial: same prefix, different unseen suffixes; compare every output before the allowed boundary; include arbitrary normalization state, chunk sizes, warm-up and left/right synchronization. Passing finite probes alone still needs an architecture audit. If the actual checkpoint is noncausal, classify it as an **offline reference** and train/obtain a causal version; changing a constructor flag cannot be assumed to preserve its listening benefit. This targets B3 first, before expending effort on B7 power projections.

**Executed analysis: the proposed diffuse bench stimulus has a geometry ambiguity.** J3 §B.3.2 allows a horizontal loudspeaker ring and compares magnitude-squared coherence to a spherical `sinc(kd)` reference. I computed the population cross-spectrum of independent equal-power plane waves for 8/32/4096 directions and three hypothetical microphone spacings. For the ideal spherical field, the normalized complex coherence is `sin(kd)/(kd)` and the **magnitude-squared** reference is its square. A horizontal ring samples a different angular distribution and converges to a different result.

At the explicitly assumed 20-mm spacing, 4 kHz and sound speed 343 m/s: spherical MSC = **0.46049**, eight-direction ring = **0.28198**, 4096-direction ring = **0.28197**. Increasing the number of ring sources does not solve the mismatch. Full sweep: [coherence.csv](../research/unstick2/coherence.csv). This is an original T0 calculation, not measured head/room/processor acoustics. Before accepting or rejecting babble, define its intended dimensionality and compare the correct squared quantity. The ambiguous recipe could otherwise reject a correctly rendered planar field. No acceptance tolerance or actual processor spacing was invented.
